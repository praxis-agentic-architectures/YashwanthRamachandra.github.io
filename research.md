## Research vision

My research direction is grounded in a practical question: how can AI systems integrate heterogeneous clinical evidence and support decisions while remaining reliable, interpretable, and accountable? I am especially interested in systems that combine medical images, clinical language, structured health data, and external knowledge without obscuring uncertainty or displacing human judgment.

> This page describes an emerging research agenda. It does not claim completed clinical studies or deployment.

## Guiding research questions

- How can clinical AI agents reason safely over longitudinal patient information?
- How can multimodal models jointly interpret medical images, clinical text, structured EHR data, and medical knowledge?
- How should clinical AI agents be evaluated for uncertainty, fairness, reliability, and failure recovery?
- How can AI augment clinicians while preserving transparency, oversight, and accountability?
- Which evaluation designs best predict whether a prototype will remain useful under real workflow constraints?

## Research themes

### 1. Agentic AI for clinical decision support

Tool-using systems that decompose clinical tasks, retrieve evidence, maintain context, and expose intermediate reasoning artifacts for review.

### 2. Multimodal clinical AI

Learning and reasoning across imaging, narrative notes, structured observations, and medical knowledge while accounting for missing and conflicting evidence.

### 3. Medical imaging and language models

Methods that connect visual findings with clinical language for grounded summarization, question answering, and decision support.

### 4. Generative AI and retrieval-augmented reasoning

Retrieval and generation pipelines designed around provenance, evidence quality, abstention, and reproducible evaluation.

### 5. Trustworthy AI, evaluation, and safety

Evaluation beyond aggregate accuracy: calibration, robustness, subgroup performance, traceability, human factors, and safe failure behavior.

### 6. Healthcare workflow intelligence

AI systems shaped by the temporal, organizational, and sociotechnical realities of care delivery rather than isolated benchmark tasks.

## Research roadmap

| Horizon | Priorities |
| --- | --- |
| Near term | Structured literature reviews, prototype development, evaluation frameworks, and reproducible experiments |
| Medium term | Open technical reports, publication-oriented studies, reusable implementations, and collaboration with healthcare researchers |
| Long term | Clinically useful, carefully evaluated AI systems that support accountable human decision-making |

## Working principles

1. **Clinical relevance before novelty.** Begin with a consequential workflow or decision problem.
2. **Evaluation is part of the design.** Define expected behavior, failure modes, and evidence requirements early.
3. **Uncertainty should be visible.** Systems should communicate limitations and abstain when evidence is insufficient.
4. **Reproducibility builds trust.** Document data assumptions, prompts, retrieval settings, model versions, and analysis methods.
5. **Human oversight is a system property.** Design review, escalation, and accountability into the workflow.
